# The MoE board — E19 resolved: it was a units bug, not a model bug

> **Additional comparator qualification, 2026-09-04:** correcting the reported
> auxiliary-loss units does not establish task-trained expert specialization.
> The [architecture review](architecture-review-2026-09-04/README.md) reproduces
> near-zero task-loss gradient at the top-1 router after selected-probability
> normalization. The one-expert control cannot detect that issue. Multi-expert
> task-only CE needs its actual auxiliary contribution; the historical numerical
> reconstruction below must not be used to conclude that extra capacity cannot help.
>
> **2026-09-05:** `Config.moe_router_weight` (`"renorm"`, the default and what every run
> below used; `"raw"` scales the chosen expert by its softmax probability, Switch-style,
> which is what gives a top-1 router a task gradient) and the arms `moe_e1k1_raw`,
> `moe_e4k1_raw`, `moe_e8k1_raw` are committed (`1b6c5d0`). Every number on this page,
> including the `moe32c` re-run, is a balance-only router. The re-run with a task-trained
> router is E29 (`crossover50m_moe32d`; handoff §5); it is readable only if
> `moe_e1k1_raw` lands inside `attention`'s interval, exactly as here. One sprint
> measurement already shows the router matters for cost: on real corpus batches
> `moe_e8k1_raw` runs 44.2K tok/s against `moe_e8k1`'s 38.4K — a router that trains
> dispatches differently.

Written 2026-09-04, superseding the reading recorded in `da11013`
("E19b: the zero-init fix was real but was not the cause; MoE stays
uninterpretable"). The cause is now found, and the board is readable.

## What E19 was for

E18's looped arms hold compute and *remove* parameters (123.7M → 59.9M). A top-1
MoE holds active compute and *adds* them (123.7M → 520.1M). Together the two
boards span 59.9M–520.1M parameters at one compute budget, which makes them a
parameter axis rather than two unrelated boards.

`moe_e1k1` is the control. At `n_exp=1` with top-1 routing, the softmax is over
a single logit and is therefore exactly 1.0, so the layer *is* its one expert: a
dense SwiGLU wearing the MoE code path. It **had** to match `attention`.

It did not. It lost by 0.127 nats with intervals disjoint from `attention` —
twice, on two separate boards.

## Two defects, and the control caught both

**Defect 1 — the zero-init did not reach MoE experts.**
`_zero_init_output_projections` tested `ffn.down`, which a MoE does not have; its
projections live on `ffn.experts[i].down`. Every MoE run ever made in this repo
trained without the identity-init stabilizer every dense arm gets.

Real, and worth the fix. **But not the gap:** re-running the entire board on the
fix (E19b) moved the control from 4.3464 to 4.3375 — about 0.009 of the 0.127.

**Defect 2 — the load-balancing aux was added to the *reported* loss.**
`Model.forward` returned `loss + moe_aux_weight * Σ_blocks aux`, and
`train.evaluate` reports exactly what `forward` returns. So every MoE arm's
`final_val` carried a term no dense arm pays.

For the 1-expert control that term is analytic. With `E=1`, every token routes to
expert 0, so `frac = [1.0]` and `mean_prob = [1.0]`, giving
`aux = E · Σ_e frac_e · p̄_e = 1.0` per block, exactly, independent of the data:

```
moe_aux_weight × n_layer  =  0.01 × 12  =  0.12 nats
```

against an observed gap of 0.127. Verified directly on CPU: with dense and
`moe_e1k1` loaded from identical weights, the evaluated losses differ by exactly
`0.01 × n_layer` and the cross-entropy underneath is bit-identical. The control's
CE was dense all along.

## The corrected board (50M tokens, n=5, E19b)

`attention` half-width is 0.0276, which is the scale to read the last column at.

| arm | params | reported `final_val` | aux term | corrected CE | vs `attention` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `attention` | 123.7M | 4.2182 [4.1907, 4.2458] | — | 4.2182 | — |
| `moe_e1k1` | 123.7M | 4.3375 [4.3108, 4.3641] | 0.1200 **exact** | **4.2175** | **−0.0008** |
| `moe_e4k1` | 256.2M | 4.3473 [4.3175, 4.3770] | ≥ 0.12 | ≤ 4.2273 | ≤ +0.0090 |
| `moe_e8k1` | 520.1M | 4.3542 [4.3260, 4.3824] | ≥ 0.12 | ≤ 4.2342 | ≤ +0.0160 |

**The control lands 0.0008 nats from `attention`** — zero to three decimal
places, against a half-width of 0.028. The board is a parameter experiment
again.

### Why only the control is exact

For `E > 1` the aux depends on how balanced the router actually was, and
**that number was never recorded**. Its range is
`aux ∈ [1, E]` per block: 1.0 at perfect balance, `E` at full collapse. So the
correction is `0.01 · Σ aux ∈ [0.12, 0.12·E]`, and only the *upper* bound on the
corrected CE is tight.

Stated honestly, the board supports:

> At 50M tokens and matched active compute, going from 1 to 8 experts —
> 123.7M → 520.1M parameters, 4.2× — costs **at most 0.016 nats**, which is
> inside the noise band of the dense reference.

It does **not** support a claim that MoE is *better*, even though the loose lower
bound permits it. Recovering the point estimate needs the aux value, which is
now logged (`val_aux`) but was not then.

The expectation that the routers were near-balanced — the aux gradient pushes
toward uniform usage for the whole run — is **inferred**, not measured, and is
exactly the assumption the missing metric would have replaced.

## Fixes landed

- `Model.forward` gates the aux on `self.training`: training pays the
  regularizer, evaluation reports pure cross-entropy. `self.training` is the
  right seam rather than a call-site change, because `train.evaluate` already
  wraps in `model.eval()`/`model.train()` — so all seven reporting paths in the
  repo (train, sft, schedules, bench_gpu, probe_perf, mqar_suite,
  test_gpu_bundle) are corrected at once, and no call site can drop the
  balancing pressure by forgetting to add a term back.
- `Model.aux_loss` exposes the weighted term; `train.evaluate` averages it and
  logs `val_aux` for MoE runs. E19 was unrecoverable after the fact **only**
  because no metric carried this number.
- `nanolab/tests.py::an_evaluated_moe_loss_is_pure_cross_entropy` asserts both
  directions: an evaluated 1-expert MoE equals dense, *and* a training-mode one
  still carries `0.01 × n_layer`. Against the pre-fix model it fails by exactly
  0.0400 at `n_layer=4`.

## The re-run, measured (2026-09-04, `crossover50m_moe32c`, 20 jobs)

Run on the fixed reporting path, so `final_val` is pure cross-entropy and needs
no subtraction. `val_aux` is now recorded rather than assumed:

| arm | params | `final_val` [95% t] | `val_aux` | vs `attention` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `moe_e1k1` | 123.7M | 4.2185 [4.1898, 4.2471] | 0.1200 | −0.0013 overlap |
| `attention` | 123.7M | 4.2198 [4.1925, 4.2470] | — | — |
| `moe_e4k1` | 256.2M | 4.2282 [4.1995, 4.2569] | 0.1201 | +0.0084 overlap |
| `moe_e8k1` | 520.1M | 4.2338 [4.2057, 4.2618] | 0.1202 | +0.0140 overlap |

**The control matches.** `moe_e1k1` lands 0.0013 nats from `attention` with
overlapping intervals, where the pre-fix board had it 0.127 away and disjoint.
The diagnosis is confirmed end to end on GPU, not just on CPU.

**`val_aux` closes the bound.** It reads 0.1200–0.1202 for every arm, including
8 experts — the perfect-balance value `moe_aux_weight × n_layer = 0.12` to four
decimals. So the routers did not collapse, the correction really was ~0.12 for
every arm, and the loose lower bound in the table above becomes a point
estimate. That number is exactly what the old board could not supply.

The correction predicted from E19b (4.2175 / 4.2273 / 4.2342) matches what was
measured directly (4.2185 / 4.2282 / 4.2338) to within 0.001 nats on all three
arms — an independent check that the constant was the whole of the artifact.

**The result, now supported rather than bounded:** at 50M tokens and matched
active compute, 1 → 8 experts — 123.7M → 520.1M parameters, **4.2×** — costs
**+0.014 nats**, with intervals overlapping the dense reference at every arm.
Parameters bought nothing measurable here. The monotone drift (+0.008, +0.014)
is in the *worse* direction and is inside the noise, so it is not evidence of a
small gain either.

## What a further re-run would buy, and what it would not

Nothing, at this budget. The point estimates and the router balance are now
measured. The open question the board cannot answer is whether 4.2x the
parameters pays off at a token budget where 50M is not the binding constraint --
MoE's case is usually made at far longer horizons, and nothing here speaks to
that.

## The general lesson, which is the paper's own

A metric that silently contains a *training* term is a recipe axis nobody
declared. Every MoE number in this repo was directly comparable to every other
MoE number and to nothing else — and the comparison it was actually used for was
against dense arms. The control is what made that visible, and the control was
cheap: one arm out of four.
